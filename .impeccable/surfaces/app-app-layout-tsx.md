THESIS: Tempo becomes an editorial ink studio for training work: sessions are
study tiles and connected marks printed onto a Monday-first paper field. It
refuses the generic dark dashboard and keeps the week, next action, and session
states immediately scannable.

OWN-WORLD: Warm paper carries the signed-in workspace. Deep indigo owns action
and active context; blue-gray linework carries organic ink flow; muted green,
ochre, clay, and violet explain training states. Navigation is a quiet studio
index with line icons, controls are square editorial instruments, and content
uses fine rules, study tiles, and authored flow linework.

STORY: An athlete enters the studio, sees the one session that matters next,
reads the Monday-first week as a connected current, and moves into the session
without hunting for the control. Completed, adjusted, missed, and active states
remain explicit in words as well as color. The surface should feel crafted and
alive through line, focus, hover, and selection, never in a way that delays
operation.

FIRST VIEWPORT: The desktop frame opens with a narrow paper navigation rail at
left and a wide editorial field at right. A circular ink mark, Tempo wordmark,
line icons, and the account menu sit in the rail. The main field
starts with a large thin greeting over authored indigo flow linework, followed
by the next session and a connected Monday-first week ribbon. Supporting totals
become distinct study tiles below. On mobile the rail becomes the existing
drawer and bottom navigation; the next action stays first and the week ribbon
scrolls horizontally without losing day labels or action controls.

FORM: Ink Is Movement is the user's supplied visual reference translated into
the product's existing operation model. The supplied Suimen-like composition
sets the paper, indigo, studio rail, oversized title, organic linework, and study
tile grammar. Product truth supplies every workout, coach, planner, calendar,
completion, and editor behavior. The form is a whole signed-in shell world, not
a route-local experiment.
